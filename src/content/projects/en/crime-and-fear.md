Two self-contained analyses asking the same questions of Germany and of Switzerland: what is recorded by the police, what is cleared, and how safe do people feel? The data come from the police crime statistics of the German BKA and the Swiss Federal Statistical Office, the Swiss population register, the European Social Survey (rounds 1–11, the same instrument for both countries), the Swiss Crime Survey 2022, and the 2015 Swiss Security Survey. The cross-country comparison uses Eurostat's harmonised offence classification, because the two national statistics draw the boundaries differently.

Every figure carries its source, its case count and its limits: confidence intervals are shown, the maps shade by rank rather than by equal value steps, and the Swiss fear figures apply to the seven major regions, since no cantonally representative survey values exist. Both editions are recorded-crime data only – what is counted is what was reported or found by the police, not what happened.
